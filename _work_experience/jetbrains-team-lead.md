My responsibilities as team lead included setting the research agenda, hiring, conducting one-on-ones, and coaching. 
Over almost two years, I focused on faster experimentation, stronger infrastructure, and research novelty, helping the team progress from occasional workshop papers to NeurIPS publications such as [LibEvoBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25402 "LibEvoBench: Probing Temporal Knowledge Stratification in Code Generation Models"). The team grew from seven to eleven people, and we moved from standalone GPU machines to managing a GPU cluster.
Our research focused primarily on adapting models to specific code repositories, with a strong emphasis on 
[measuring coding capabilities](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=dQs1srLmGN "Don’t Claim Benchmark-Oriented Optimization Improves General Coding Capability — Diverse Evaluation Is Required"). This work led to a shift toward multi-task learning, focusing on adding new capabilities during post-training and understanding how reasoning affects performance across tasks.
