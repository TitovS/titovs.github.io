After leading several successful projects with teams of two to three people, I became the Code Modeling team lead, initially managing seven researchers. My responsibilities included setting the research agenda, hiring, conducting one-on-ones, and coaching. Over almost two years, I focused on faster experimentation, stronger infrastructure, and research novelty, helping the team progress from occasional workshop papers to NeurIPS publications. The team grew from seven to eleven people, and we moved from standalone GPU machines to managing a GPU cluster.

During this period, our research focused primarily on adapting models to specific code repositories, with a strong emphasis on measuring coding capabilities. This work led to a shift toward multi-task learning, focusing on adding new capabilities during post-training and understanding how reasoning affects performance across tasks.
